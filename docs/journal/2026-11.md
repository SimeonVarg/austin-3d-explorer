# Austin 3D Explorer — journal, November 2026

## Nov 12 2026 — the clean still: at rest, thin bars are whole lines and edges are smooth (#680)

**What changed for the eye.** On the owner's screen (one canvas pixel per CSS pixel, the multisampled path), when the camera
stops, the picture cleans itself in about one second: a window bar that was a row of dots becomes a whole line, a stair on an
edge becomes smooth. Nothing changes while the camera moves.

**How (`v2/rest.js`).** When nothing else needs drawing, the app draws the same view 16 more times, each moved by a part of a
pixel (Halton, over the whole pixel), into a multisampled target that is named a screen, and adds the frames up as light. The
first frame added is the unshifted one, so nothing jumps; each later frame counts for 1 / (frames so far). After the 16th the
page draws nothing. Any motion or change of content draws a plain frame and starts again. Fast mode only; off under a test
driver (`?rest=1` turns it on there); a screen on the steady picture keeps its own still.

**Measured (owner's Mac, 2560 x 1300, ratio 1, against a 16-sample truth).** The still's error: 1.75 -> 0.33 of 255 (downtown
view), 1.62 -> 0.30 (campus view); on the live site, as a person gets it, 0.44. Complete 1.0 to 1.3 s after a stop. The
opening flight is as on main (median frame 45 ms). Check: `scripts/verify/v2-rest.mjs` (both renderers).

**What it does not do.** The same thin parts still break into dots while the camera moves.

## Nov 12 2026 — 25 far copies that did not look like their buildings are switched off (#679)

**The fault.** The far copies (#671, live since Nov 11) were tested by an average over whole frames. Ranking today's flicker by
class at the owner's screen size showed the far copies' pixels three times as far from a full-model truth as any other class,
and a look showed why: beyond the far copy's line the William P. Clements building was a dark blotched block, the Moody
Center's white roof had black shards, the stadium's green field was pale, a dark tower by the Marriott was light.

**The scan (`scripts/verify/v2-far-scan.mjs`, new).** Per tile, in one page: the camera 1.2 x the line away, the tile drawn as
full model, as far copy and hidden; over the tile's own pixels `err` = mean |far - full| of 255, `bright` = the signed
brightness change, `over30` = the share of pixels more than 30 off; two sides (azimuth 200 and 20), the worse counts. All 87:
err median 7.2 (5 to 7 is the full model's own fine detail against a filtered copy), six tiles from 12.7 to 21.0.

**What changed.** A far copy with err over 9.0, or over30 at 8 % or more, or |bright| over 5 is `"off"` in
`v2/tiles/coats.json` (25 tiles; `v2/tiles.js` skips it, the full model draws at every distance), and every entry carries its
numbers under `"scan"`. Cost on the owner's screen: the flight's median frame 44-48 ms -> 47-50 ms (about 6 %).

**The cause, found the same day (not in #679).** In `scripts/v2/far_coat.py` a shell sample that lies just UNDER its surface
(the shell is a loose skin, and a ray starts only half way to the next shell layer in front) fell through the hall below and
was painted as "a cap over a void", dark. With a closest-surface test first, the Moody Center's far copy scans at 4.2 and 2.9
instead of 21.0 and 20.3. A rebake of all 87 with that fix follows in its own pull request; a far copy comes back on only
when it passes the scan from both sides.

## Nov 11 2026 — the steady picture: full size in motion on the owner's Mac, at the same frame rate (#674)

**What changed for the eye.** On his Mac the city was drawn with a quarter of the pixels while the camera moved, and snapped back to
sharp at every stop after a pause of 150 to 350 ms. Now the picture stays at FULL size while it moves, and a stop settles with no
jump and no pause. The opening flight runs at the same rate (29.4 to 29.7 frames a second against 30.4 to 30.5, same day, in turn).

**Measured first (branch `mac/v2-motion-pixels`).** One flat material at the canvas's full size, no multisampling: 10 to 12 ms. So
the triangles were never the cost. The real materials at full size: 52 to 58 ms. The cost is the fragment shaders, spread over every
layer; and the canvas's own multisampling is 10 ms of a 32 ms motion frame on this GPU. Full size in every frame is out of reach;
so the only way to SHOW 5.2 M sharp pixels 30 times a second is not to shade all of them in every frame.

**How (`v2/steady.js`).** The city stands still and so does the sun, so a point seen in this frame can be found again in the last
frame's picture exactly, from its depth and the two cameras. The scene is drawn small (a quarter of the pixels, no multisampling),
shifted by a different part of a pixel each frame, into a target that three.js treats as the screen. At full size each pixel finds
its own surface, takes the KEPT picture from where that point was one frame ago, and mixes the new sample in as light. The kept
picture is copied to the canvas, which never changes size. At rest the same loop adds shifted full-size frames, each at a small
share (a large share made the still flicker while it settled: 1.5 to 2.6 of 255 a frame, now 0.1 to 0.2), and the still converges to
a supersampled picture (1.1 of 255 from a 16-sample truth; the multisampled still of before: 1.5). In the small frame
every pixel-size rule and mip level acts as for the full-size pixel (`v2PxD`), or the small frames hold no fine detail to add up.

**Three faults, each found from a picture that was wrong.** (1) No ground layer of the app writes depth (the aerial photograph, the
streets and the markings are flat layers), so half the picture was carried with the wrong parallax: the ground's depth is now the
plane y = 0. (2) A pixel beside an edge took the nearest sample's depth: the ground around every tree smeared. Each pixel now asks
the kept picture which of three depths fits. (3) "A kept depth anywhere between the neighbours' depths is good" left a trail of
every building on the ground it uncovered at speed: the kept depth must now fit the surface, with room for the surface's slope.
The carry test (a still at camera A, one motion frame at camera B that takes nothing new, against a still at B) found (1) and (3).

**Numbers (owner's Mac, real GPU, 1440 x 900 at 2x, recorded opening flight, against a 16-sample truth of each frame; error / error
an eye resolves / sharpness / shimmer).** At 6.5 s: 5.55 / 2.92 / 0.66 / 3.98 before, 2.74 / 1.27 / 0.88 / 2.56 now. At 9.3 s (the
fastest part): 4.30 / 2.40 / 0.75 / 6.71 before, 4.12 / 2.78 / 0.89 / 6.56 now (equal: there the new frame counts for more). At
11.6 s: 4.39 / 2.46 / 0.78 / 6.12 before, 2.96 / 1.79 / 0.90 / 4.27 now. The stop: one jump of 6.5 of 255 before, 2.2 in all now, over 24 frames with no pause.
A full-size multisampled frame every time (not reachable) would be about 1.3 / 0.6 / 1.05 / 1.9.

**Who gets it.** A device that learned a motion scale under 1 on an earlier visit, or on a first visit a GPU built into an Intel
processor (`QUALITY.STEADY_GPU`). A GPU that holds the full size keeps the old path, and so does a phone. `?steady=0` is the old
path (a motion frame equal to main's to the last pixel), `?steady=1` forces the new one.

**Not solved, said plainly.** At the fastest part of the flight it is equal to before, not better. The bridge cables write no
depth and are carried with what is behind them. A first visit on a weak GPU that is not Intel starts on the old path. The owner
has not judged it yet; his eye in the opening flight is the acceptance (rule 15).

**Checks.** New `scripts/verify/v2-steady.mjs` (in CI: still, carry, motion, rest). The 12 checks that draw frames themselves open
the page with `steady=0` (`scripts/verify/README.md` says why). On the Mac: v2-steady, v2-light, v2-labels, v2-label-twins,
v2-collision-tops, v2-flycam, v2-tile-pokes, v2-sky-shimmer, v2-full-detail (same numbers as main), v2-demo-checks: pass.

**The truth test (`scripts/verify/v2-full-detail.mjs`, branch `mac/v2-fast-default`).** The truth of a view = every tile as its full
model, drawn with 3 x 3 samples a pixel, the samples added up as light. The app's frame is scored against it over the pixels where the
far copies change anything: brightness drift, and the difference after a 3 x 3 average (what an eye resolves). It replaces the check
"no far copy at any distance" (owner, 2026-10-18) with what that order was about: a building must not LOOK less detailed at a distance.

**Fault 1: the far copies were pale.** Window rows washed out of every white tower. An hour went to wrong suspects (rays through
openings, caps over voids, tilted shell faces, the mirror glass). Drawing each shader term raw on the screen found it: the texture was
right, each light term was right, the SUM was wrong only after the screen mapping. The app clips bright light (Blender Standard). A
white wall in the sun is over the clip, so a texel that held wall and dark pane as one mixed colour was still over the clip and drew
white. clip(mix) is not mix(clip). **Model 3:** the far copy's picture holds the bright part's colour (rgb) and the dark part's share
(alpha, linear); the dark part's colour and mirror are one value a face; the shader sends the two parts to the screen apart and mixes
them there. Samples are parted per shell face by brightness (Otsu), and a mirror pane is always of the dark part. All 87 far copies
were rebuilt (27.1 MB). `coats.json` entries carry `model`; the app draws only its own model.

**Fault 2: with the tree cards no tree had a shadow** (live behind `?fast=1` since #669, and in the clip the owner got that morning).
three.js draws the BACK faces of a front-sided mesh into the shadow map; a card turned to the sun has none. One line in `v2/trees.js`.

**Numbers (owner's Mac, real GPU; the truth test at 1000 x 640).** Brightness drift of the app's frame from the truth: opening view

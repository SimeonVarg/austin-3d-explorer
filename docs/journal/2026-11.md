## Nov 11 2026 — the fast mode is the default on a desktop, after a truth test found it pale and without tree shadows (#670, #671)

**The owner's words (text, 09:50).** "If it's faster why ask me to keep or throw away? I would throw away faster option when you didn't
give me any downsides? More input from me for no reason. I asked what you are working on I didn't get a clear answer". So the fast mode
(#668, #669) was to become the default with no question. It did, but only after two faults in it were found and fixed the same day.

**The truth test (`scripts/verify/v2-full-detail.mjs`, branch `mac/v2-fast-default`).** The truth of a view = every tile as its full
model, drawn with 3 x 3 samples a pixel, the samples added up as light. The app's frame is scored against it over the pixels where the
far copies change anything: brightness drift, and the difference after a 3 x 3 average (what an eye resolves). It replaces the check
"no far copy at any distance" (owner, 2026-10-18) with what that order was about: a building must not LOOK less detailed at a distance.

**Fault 1: the far copies were pale.** Window rows washed out of every white tower. An hour went to wrong suspects (rays through
openings, caps over voids, tilted shell faces, the mirror glass). Drawing each shader term raw on the screen found it: the texture was
right, each light term was right, the SUM was wrong only after the screen mapping. The app clips bright light (Blender Standard). A
white wall in the sun is over the clip, so a texel that held wall and dark pane as one mixed colour was still over the clip and drew
white. clip(mix) is not mix(clip). **Model 3:** the far copy's picture holds the bright part's colour (rgb) and the dark part's share
(alpha, linear); the dark part's colour and mirror are one value a face; the shader sends the two parts to the screen apart and mixes
them there. Samples are parted per shell face by brightness (Otsu), and a mirror pane is always of the dark part. All 87 far copies
were rebuilt (27.1 MB). `coats.json` entries carry `model`; the app draws only its own model.

**Fault 2: with the tree cards no tree had a shadow** (live behind `?fast=1` since #669, and in the clip the owner got that morning).
three.js draws the BACK faces of a front-sided mesh into the shadow map; a card turned to the sun has none. One line in `v2/trees.js`.

**Numbers (owner's Mac, real GPU; the truth test at 1000 x 640).** Brightness drift of the app's frame from the truth: opening view
1.36 of 255, DKR from 2 km 1.21 (limit 3; that morning's far copy: 4.7 to 4.9 in an older, harsher form of the measure); "seen" 5.9 and
6.5 (limit 8; all-full at the app's resolution: 2.3). On the software renderer: drift 1.4 and 0.9. Tree cards against the truth: drift
-1.2 and -0.5 (round crowns as balls: -0.75 and -0.57). Opening flight, 1440 x 900 at 2x, fresh page each run, interleaved, machine
load about 8: default 20.2 to 20.8 frames a second (median frame 39 to 40 ms, 1.2 M triangles), `?fast=0` 12.4 to 13.3 (64 to 67 ms,
7.7 M triangles). In motion the canvas is still at pixel ratio 1.0: smoother, not sharper.

**Known and measured, not fixed.** Grey mid-tone parts are averaged into the bright part and clip with it: white sunlit window faces
are 3 to 5 of 255 too bright in the far copy. In motion the full models between the motion line (about 1.1 km) and the rest line
(about 2.2 km) keep most of their baked AO and the far copies have none. A phone keeps the old path: the far copies are a second set
of textures (about 190 MB on the GPU) and a phone's memory is not measured.

**Also:** #670 (a far copy is never drawn for a tile rebuilt after it: `tile_bytes` in `coats.json`). `AGENTS.md` no longer has the
same text as `CLAUDE.md` (516 differing lines on this day); it was not touched.

## Nov 11 2026 — the opening flight measured as it really runs; far copies, tree cards, steady motion resolution (#668, #669)

**What was found.** On the owner's Mac the opening flight runs at pixel ratio 1.0 (a quarter of the pixels) from 2.8 s to its end, at
11 to 20 frames a second, 4 to 11 M triangles a frame. Every shimmer gate before measured still views at full resolution. The cost of a
frame there is the number of triangles (tiles squeezed to zero size cost almost the same), and each change of canvas resolution is a
stop of 150 to 350 ms.

**What was made (branches `mac/v2-far-coat`, `mac/v2-tree-cards`).**
- A far copy of every tile (`scripts/v2/far_coat.py`, `v2/tiles/*.coat.glb`, `v2/tiles.js` FAR): a shell of what is visible, painted
  with the model's own colours and its mirror strength per texel. Standing still the full models draw as before (out to about 2.2 km);
  in motion the far copies draw beyond about 1.1 km. Switch `?far=1`, off by default.
- Tree cards (`?treecards=1`, off): a far crown is one card on which the pixel shader draws the exact ellipsoid.
- On for everyone, the same picture: the sky dome cut to what can show; the plain city blocks joined per 900 m square.
- `?adaptup=0` (off): no step up in resolution in the middle of a motion; the motion resolution is kept on the device.
- `?fast=1` turns the three switches on together.

**Measured (the real flight).** main: 12.8 frames a second, median frame 65 ms, 5.7 s of 12.7 s in frames over 85 ms, 7.8 M triangles.
`?fast=1`: 24.0 to 24.6 frames a second, median 38 ms, 0.7 to 0.8 s, 1.2 M triangles. Full models against far copies at three flight cameras in the motion state: mean difference 0.3 to 0.8 of 255. Far copies from 740 m in place of 1.1 km doubled that difference for a small gain, so the line stays. Still pixel ratio 1.0 in motion: ground, sky and
plain blocks alone are 24 to 28 ms a frame there.

**Not decided.** The owner removed the old far copies on 2026-10-18. These go on by default only on his eye.

**Merged:** #669 (aa972cb1) and #668 (776f3def), both with every check green and 0 of 10 views different from main. Tools for the measuring are private and untracked (`scripts/verify/_*.local.mjs`). A data fault found on the way: `the-venue` and `the-venue-on-guadalupe` are the same model at the same place in tile t_-1_-4.

## Nov 10 2026 — photo skins for Rowling Hall and the AT&T Center entrance (#666, `mac/v2-skin-batch1`)

- **Live** (tile `t_-1_1` and the atlas on the Pages mirror and the host equal `main`; the live site looked at from the photograph's camera). Rowling: the court side (fins, glass front, lit ground floor, entrance). AT&T: the stone entrance bay, window, lanterns, steps.
- **Bake changes:** the colour factor is the sum of the build's wall colours over the sum of what the photographs show (a mean of ratios gave Rowling 0.91 where 0.42 is right); the build's own parts (signs, lamps, rails) are masked in each photograph, so a sign is not drawn twice; a bake may set its own texture bias (Rowling 0).
- **Shimmer meter, main / branch:** Rowling east 250 m 0.15 / 0.11, east 600 m 0.58 / 0.44, south-east 250 m 0.11 / 0.13; AT&T equal on all four views.

- **What the method is good for:** parts with detail that does not repeat (entrances, carved stone, glass with rooms behind it, cornices), from a close daytime photograph with no lamps on.

## Nov 10 2026 — photo skin: walls keep the pixels of their photographs (#665, `mac/v2-photo-skin`)

- **What:** `scripts/v2/photo_skin.mjs` (bake on the Mac, about a minute, no model call; apply in the tile build). A building's
  faces are cut into flat rectangles; each texel takes its colour from the photograph that sees it best (depth test, tree/sky/car
  masks, one trusted photograph per rectangle, tone-matched fill for what no photograph saw, small relief drawn with its wall).
  Each wall is kept as sharp as its photograph was there. The tile holds the atlas at half size; `v2/tiles.js` loads the full one

## Oct 4 2026 - the weak tier follows the settings rules, and Safari on the Intel Mac measured (`acer/intel-mac-speed`, PR #390, open, not merged)

**What changed.** `main` (#391: settings revision 4, per-key validation, the
`autoDownAt` stamp and its 3-day expiry) is merged into the weak-tier branch and
the `js/graphics.js` conflict is resolved by hand, keeping both. The weak tier is
an automatic downgrade, so it now: carries and renews the stamp; is put back to
"all" when it has no stamp and is not custom (a hand-picked Performance); drops a
nonsense saved value and voids the judged flag; and is never overridden when the
preset or slider was chosen by hand. A hand pick used to be probed into the weak
tier; that is fixed.

**Expiry, and why.** When the stamp runs out a saved weak tier is NOT thrown back
to Balanced. The page loads in the saved tier (one build) and the first probe
measures it: over `WEAK_TIER.keepAboveMs` (= `PROBE.slowMs`, 21.5 ms) it is kept
and the stamp renewed with nothing rebuilt; at or under it the page climbs to
Balanced and the ordinary probe judges from there. Going back to Balanced would
cost a weak machine the whole descent again (in Safari the second step alone
arrives 3.5 to 4 minutes after reveal). A stamped Performance never judged for the
weak tier stays Performance and arms the probe for that step alone.

**Checks.** `edge-defaults.mjs` (about 40 new assertions, mutation-checked),
`audit-settings.mjs` (89, 0 failing), `stored-state.mjs` (13 scenarios, 66 checks,
all passed; `--break` still red; the #391 `fresh` seed now says `weakChecked: true`
and the old shape is the new `unjudged` scenario). Strong GPU, Balanced, frozen
camera: main against branch differs by 0.006 to 0.009 of 255, the same as main
against main.

**Safari 17.6 on the Intel Mac** (first time; one visible tab, window 2560 x 1360
at devicePixelRatio 2, canvas 3840 x 2040 at Performance, renderer reads "Apple
GPU"). A real visit, nothing forced. Main ends in Performance at 5.3 fps (187 ms
median frame, p95 208 ms). The branch reaches the weak tier through its own probe
(204, 227 and 245 s after reveal) and runs 5.8 fps (173 ms, p95 192 ms): about 9
percent, not the 18 to 27 fps of headless Chrome. Balanced is 3.9 fps on both. A
saved weak tier loads in one build and settles in 25 s; a 30-day-old stamp is
measured at once, kept and renewed (27 s). A diagnostic that forced the render
scale after settling gave main 5.8 fps at 3.5 MP and 6.1 fps at 1.25 MP: the
frame floor in Safari is neither the apartment triangles nor the pixels. **Next:
find it** (layer-by-layer ablation in the same harness) before cutting more
detail.

**Harness traps from this pass.** A hidden Safari page gets no animation frames,
so the probe cannot run and a 10 minute settle can produce nothing; check
visibility at the start of every step, and bring the tab forward only when the
Mac's keyboard and mouse have been idle. A background shell that serves a test
page dies at its time limit (default 30 minutes) mid-run; give servers the longest
timeout. Two beacons posted back to back to one file can overwrite each other's
head: await the result before posting the next.

  puts both back when the mesh stops. NHB uses `'nhb'`.

**Checked in the real app** (no data swaps, branch vs main, balanced and
performance presets): 0 errors, each building built once, 0 rendered NHB

**Buildings from street photographs: the owner's verdict.** Only two
changes passed his look: #383 2400 Nueces (merged) and #379 Ion Austin
(merge once its CI is green). #380 Moontower, #381 21 Rio, #382 Villas on
24th, #384 Signature 1909, #385 The Mark and #386 The Otis Hotel were
closed. His reasons: they did not capture the building's depth or colour,
and the cameras did not match the photographs. The bar is "looks like the
building in the photograph", not "closer than before".

**The cameras were wrong, and that is fixed upstream of this repo.** The
render cameras came from an old fitter that guessed the tilt from a coarse
grid (21 Rio: 25 degrees, the photograph's own vertical lines say 35), and
the renderer faked looking up with MapLibre's top padding at pitch 88. That
is a shift lens: verticals stay parallel and roughly the top 12 degrees of
the photograph's view is cut off, so an upper-floor photograph was compared
with storefronts. The fix renders one tall, centred frame from the
photograph's eye that holds its whole view, then warps it into the
photograph's camera (same eye, pure rotation, so the warp is exact).
Padding is not used: MapLibre draws its sky from the unpadded centre
(`skyUniformValues` uses `height / 2`), so a padded frame shows a dark band
above the horizon. Tilt and roll now come from the photograph's vertical
lines. Position and heading are searched by rendering the app and scoring
long straight edges and the sky outline against the photograph. Method,
unchanged: a camera that does not line up is dropped, then a blind compare.
Never write photo ids, who took a photo, or camera positions into a
building file: the repo is public.
